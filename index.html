02 — How it works

One record. One query. One payment.

  1. 01

    Publish

    Add a WALLET record to your DNS: a coin and an address. Sign the zone with DNSSEC and you're done.

  2. 02

    Resolve

    Any wallet, app or AI agent asks DNS one question. The answer is cached worldwide and cryptographically signed.

  3. 03

    Pay

    USDC or SOL goes straight to the published address and settles on Solana in seconds. Robinhood Chain works too.

03 — For agents

x402 made URLs payable. 262 makes them accountable.

When an API answers 402 Payment Required, it tells your agent where to send money, and the agent just trusts it. A hijacked server or a prompt injection can swap that address.

TYPE/262 adds one check: does this address belong to the domain I'm talking to? One line of code, answered by DNS.

06 — Roadmap

Live, and next.

  1. Live

    Website coins

    Launch a coin for any app, web game or website on pump.fun, with the domain's vault as its creator, so every creator fee goes to the domain. The owner claims them with a DNSSEC-signed record. Also on pons, on Robinhood Chain. Open the launchpad →

    01
  2. Live

    A vault for every domain

    Pay any domain before it publishes: SOL, USDC or any token to its vault on Solana, or into escrow on Robinhood Chain. Owners claim instantly once their DNSSEC-signed record names a wallet; senders can refund after 180 days. Send a website money →

    02
  3. Live

    Claims checked by DNSSEC

    A claim pays only the wallet the domain's signed record names. On Solana, TYPE/262 pays a vault out once Cloudflare and Google agree on that record and both validated its signatures. On Robinhood Chain, ENS's oracle verifies them on-chain from the DNS root keys.

    03
  4. Live

    Resolve, pay, publish, verify

    Look up any name, pay it USDC or SOL from your wallet, generate your record and check x402 payTo addresses against DNS. App, SDK and CLI.

    04
  5. Next

    DNSSEC on-chain on Solana

    A Solana program that verifies DNSSEC itself, from the root keys, so Solana claims need no server: RSA in the program, P-256 and Ed25519 through Solana's native precompiles. Built and tested in the repository, ready for a later deploy.
